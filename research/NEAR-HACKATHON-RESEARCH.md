# NEAR HACKATHON: research and product opportunities

Research date: 29 October 2026. Prepared for selecting a project, before implementation. Product concepts and rankings are proposals and judgments; cited platform capabilities and event rules are researched facts. Feasibility assumes 2–4 builders and roughly two weeks. A solo builder should implement the smallest demonstrated loop and defer additional networks.

**What matters most**

This event rewards a working use of programmable money. The strongest projects make an economic interaction possible or safer: a budget shared by independent agents, a service purchased against measurable acceptance criteria, a community asset that pays for its own maintenance, or a market that coordinates people without exposing their private inputs. Adding a conversational interface to a wallet is a weaker direction because much of the underlying wallet and agent-market functionality already exists.

The research covers all 13 tracks and all 12 ecosystem partners listed by the event. Separate sponsor bounty eligibility or sponsor prize schedules were not found in the reviewed official material. Throughout this report, partner fit means a useful technical or product relationship; it does not mean a confirmed additional prize.

**How the technologies fit together**

Think of five separate responsibilities: deciding an action, authorizing it, obtaining inputs, settling money, and proving what happened. A useful architecture assigns each responsibility explicitly. An LLM can propose a purchase; a NEAR contract can reserve the budget; an attested worker can process private inputs; Intents can route payment; receipts and acceptance tests can determine whether to release a milestone.

| Primitive | Useful capability | Design boundary |
|---|---|---|
| NEAR accounts and contracts | Persistent identities, asset balances, programmable authorization and state | An account name or subaccount hierarchy alone is not a permission system. Define every privileged action. |
| Function-call keys | Delegate calls to a specified contract and optional method list | `allowance` is a gas budget, not a token spending limit. These keys cannot attach NEAR to calls. A funded policy contract must enforce value limits. |
| NEAR Intents / verifier | Signed authorizations and settlement of compatible asset balances | Atomicity inside the verifier does not make external deposits, withdrawals and arbitrary applications one atomic transaction. |
| 1Click | Quote, route and monitor cross-chain swaps with refund handling | It does not supply a business's invoice, escrow, delivery, cancellation or dispute semantics. Those are application work. |
| Chain Signatures | A NEAR account/contract can request signatures for supported external chains | Signing is outbound authorization. It is not evidence of remote balances or successful execution. Construct, broadcast and confirm target-chain transactions separately. |
| Meta transactions | A relayer pays gas on behalf of a signed user action | Costs move to a relayer; they do not disappear. Account initialization and abuse controls remain necessary. |
| Confidential AI inference | Process model inputs in a documented confidential execution tier | Model inference, application code, tools, browser logs and output disclosure have separate privacy boundaries. |
| Attested execution | Evidence about the code/environment involved in a computation | Does not prove an outside API told the truth, an LLM reached a correct conclusion, or money reached its final destination. |
| NEP-141 / NEP-171 / NEP-245 | Fungible tokens, NFT ownership and multi-token accounting | Extra mechanics, royalty compliance and NFT-controlled accounts need explicit implementation. |
| SputnikDAO / Trezu | Existing proposal, approval and treasury infrastructure | Build community spending workflows on it; avoid spending the sprint rebuilding governance plumbing. |

**Findings that change what we should build**

1. **Concurrency is a concrete agent-wallet problem.** OutLayer documents that cumulative daily/hourly/monthly caps use gateway counters and can overshoot during concurrent requests. Its per-transaction checks have a different guarantee. This creates room for a contract that reserves a shared budget before requests proceed. To make that guarantee real, every spend must pass through the reserve mechanism; merely displaying reservations alongside a bypassable wallet is insufficient. [OutLayer custody documentation](https://app.outlayer.ai/docs/agent-custody).

2. **Private AI requires choosing and verifying the right path.** NEAR AI lists native Confidential TEE, third-party Confidential TEE and Incognito models. Incognito does not put the upstream model inside a TEE. A gateway signature is also different from a provider-TEE signature. Use the documented preflight verification and retain exact request/response bytes; publish only commitments or permitted derived outputs. [Model tiers](https://docs.near.ai/cloud/models), [response verification](https://docs.near.ai/cloud/verification/cloud-api/response-signatures).

3. **Old tutorials can recommend a retired default.** Shade Agents is documented as no longer maintained as standalone tooling after April 19, 2026. Its source remains available, but a new hackathon project should not assume maintained support. Prefer current services or a small explicit worker/contract design unless an existing team already knows the framework. [Deprecation notice](https://docs.near.org/ai/shade-agents/getting-started/introduction).

4. **Fees need runtime evidence.** Hackathon FAQ Q-006 says unauthenticated 1Click costs 0.2%; current Intents documentation says an additional 25 bps on unauthenticated non-ANY_INPUT quotes and gives route-dependent authenticated schedules. Treat this as a source discrepancy; display the actual quoted input/output and itemized available fees. Do not hard-code a blanket fee claim. [Hackathon FAQ](https://nearhackathon.com/HACKATHON.md), [current fee documentation](https://docs.near-intents.org/resources/fees).

5. **A route is not a payment SLA.** Intents availability, asset support, minimums and external finality matter. Payment apps need statuses for funds reserved, sent, pending, completed, failed and refunded, with idempotent retries. Automatic timeout refunds are unsafe if an external transfer could still settle later. [1Click overview](https://docs.near-intents.org/integration/distribution-channels/1click-api/about-1click-api), [NEAR asynchronous calls](https://docs.near.org/smart-contracts/anatomy/crosscontract).

6. **NFT ownership is only the starting point.** Changing an NFT owner does not automatically revoke a former owner's subaccount keys or update an unrelated wallet. The account controller should authenticate current NFT ownership and version permissions, and pending operations must have defined transfer behavior. Standards and implementation notes are detailed in `creative-economies-research.md`.

7. **Do not confuse throughput with investment value.** A research project must separate swap volume, distribution fees, solver revenue, protocol fees, token burning and token demand. They are different cash flows and different claims. SVRN's public NEAR research supplies a useful thesis to interrogate, not proof that any particular valuation is correct. [SVRN research](https://svrn.net/research/near-protocol), [Intents fee recipients and layers](https://docs.near-intents.org/resources/fees).

**01 — Unstoppable DeFi**

Track fit: each concept changes how liquidity or a financial position is managed, with actual settlement as the proof. Detailed technical sources and longer specifications: [markets research](markets-research.md).

**D1. Margin Lifeboat — rescue a borrower's position under a strict mandate.** A user may have enough wealth overall but lack the right debt token near a deteriorating lending position. Give a rescue controller a capped budget, an approved debt position, a health threshold and a conversion-loss ceiling. The MVP can repay only that debt. Core integration: Rhea position reads/repayment plus Intents; Chain Signatures is an extension for remote reserves. The distinctive mechanism is borrower-controlled rescue authority, not another liquidator. **MVP/demo:** one position and one asset pair; move a simulated price toward liquidation, show a rejected excessive rescue, then one permitted repayment. **Risk:** bridge latency and unavailable liquidity. Keep a reserve near the debt and intervene early; do not promise liquidation insurance. **Business hypothesis:** paid monitoring and bounded execution. Secondary fit: Security.

**D2. DueDate Vault — a treasury organized around obligations.** A business inputs payroll and supplier due dates. The vault works backward from each deadline, reserving liquid funds and allowing yield exposure only for surplus money. It measures obligations that remain fundable under stress. Core integration: a NEAR obligation registry, one Rhea lending adapter and Intents payout; Trezu can approve policy changes. **MVP/demo:** three bills, one yield source, two recipient networks. Bring payroll forward and watch the system unwind discretionary exposure while explaining the remaining shortfall. **Risk:** redemptions can be illiquid and transfers delayed. Near-term obligations need a funded buffer; promised APY is irrelevant if payday is missed. **Business hypothesis:** treasury subscription. Secondary fit: Business, Real-World Money. This extends existing yield access with liability matching.

**D3. Inventory Commons — let smaller solvers rebalance together.** Several market makers have complementary inventory imbalances. A matcher finds exchange cycles among already deposited assets; each participant signs exact acceptable balance changes. Settle the complete bundle in the Intents verifier and route only unmatched residuals elsewhere. Core integration: lower-level Intents; Rhea is an optional residual-liquidity source. **MVP/demo:** three solvers and three assets; show conserved balances, a funded exchange cycle, and rejection of a stale signature. Compare against executable independent quotes. **Risk:** counterparties and inventory privacy, plus the complexity of signed settlement. The atomic promise ends at the common ledger; external withdrawals remain separate. **Business hypothesis:** a fee on completed rebalances. Secondary fit: Swarms if agents actually negotiate. This is a capital-efficiency layer for solvers, not a generic swap frontend.

**02 — Investment Thesis**

Track fit: these produce reproducible arguments, assumptions and counterarguments. A negative or conditional result can still be strong research. Detailed data-access sources: [markets research](markets-research.md).

**T1. Value Capture Microscope — test whether ecosystem growth benefits NEAR.** Build a transparent model separating gross swap volume, solver spreads, application charges, protocol fees, gas burning, storage locks and actual token demand. Ask how each responds to adoption, batching and pricing changes. Core integrations are NEAR data and Intents records; SVRN provides a published thesis to compare against. **MVP/demo:** one bounded historical sample, reproducible queries, bull/base/bear assumptions and explicit falsification thresholds. Increase volume tenfold and reveal which value-capture channels barely change. **Risk:** missing attribution; show unknown residuals and avoid treating storage locks as revenue. **Product hypothesis:** protocol-economics diligence tooling. This goes beyond charting adoption by testing the causal argument from adoption to token economics.

**T2. Settlement Weather — measure the reliability behind chain abstraction.** Treat cross-chain routes as a transport network. Measure quote availability, minimum viable transaction size, delivery-time distributions, refund rates and observed dependency concentration. Link those properties to applications that could sustainably use the network. Core integration: Intents dry quotes and available historical records. **MVP/demo:** three corridors and three sizes; show that an apparently cheap route has poor tail latency. Separate quote probes from funded outcomes. **Risk:** a short sample cannot establish long-term reliability, and Explorer coverage is limited to 1Click. **Product hypothesis:** observability for wallets and payment providers. The thesis is that reliable settlement can attract durable demand; the research must also show conditions under which it fails.

**T3. Demand Archaeology — discover what activity metrics actually represent.** Construct cohorts of observable workflows: repeated external inflows followed by payments, contract automation, circular transfers, and one-off arrivals. Test whether adoption remains when the largest applications or suspected recycled flows are excluded. Core integration: receipt-aware NEAR datasets and documented schemas for Intents/Rhea/Aurora/HOT. **MVP/demo:** two app families, a historical window and adjustable classification assumptions, all reproducible. **Risk:** addresses are not people and flow heuristics can misclassify activity. Preserve raw metrics alongside inferred cohorts; publish uncertainty. **Product hypothesis:** ecosystem diligence and growth-quality reporting. The differentiated output is a thesis that survives alternate definitions of “usage,” not another active-wallet dashboard.

Data-access gate for all three: obtain the necessary Explorer credentials and check dataset freshness immediately. Explorer requires JWT, covers only 1Click and documents a five-second request interval. NEAR's BigQuery documentation warns that the old Lake S3 ingestion stopped in March 2026. Use a frozen, dated reproducible dataset if a current feed cannot be verified. [Explorer](https://docs.near-intents.org/integration/distribution-channels/1click-api/explorer/introduction), [BigQuery](https://docs.near.org/data-infrastructure/big-query).

**03 — Privacy & Private AI**

Track fit: confidentiality enables an economic interaction that participants would otherwise avoid. Full technical references and designs: [agents and privacy research](agents-privacy-research.md).

**P1. Blind Tender — discover group purchases without revealing negotiating limits.** Buyers encrypt quantities, deadlines and budgets; suppliers encrypt tiered offers. A published deterministic algorithm clears a feasible purchasing group and reveals each participant's allocation. NEAR AI privately structures messy quotes for participant confirmation; OutLayer executes private clearing; a NEAR escrow and Intents handle funding/payout. **MVP/demo:** five buyers, three vendors and one category, with both a successful round and an infeasible round. Show that a changed computation is rejected and losing bids remain hidden. **Risk:** repeated queries and public payment metadata can leak constraints; fix rounds and minimize disclosures. **Business hypothesis:** a success fee. The innovation is private multi-attribute allocation, with AI supporting input handling rather than arbitrarily choosing winners. Secondary fit: Swarms, Business.

**P2. CleanRoom Acceptance — verify a private deliverable before buying it.** A data buyer and seller agree on tests before either commits: schema, row preservation, null limits or sample performance. An attested worker evaluates the encrypted artifact and private buyer sample, revealing only approved metrics. NEAR escrow binds payment to the artifact and evaluator version; OutLayer controls evaluation/key release. Intents is an optional payout adapter. **MVP/demo:** CSV datasets, five fixed tests, one accepted purchase and one rejected purchase. Reject modified tests; do not reveal failed deliverables. **Risk:** fair exchange requires durable key availability after payment and a recovery path if the evaluator disappears. No arbitrary hostile code in the MVP. **Business hypothesis:** per-evaluation fees. Secondary fit: Agent Economy. This sells a verifiable acceptance boundary, not generic secure storage.

**P3. Cohort Lens — private benchmarks whose contributors share the revenue.** Merchants contribute encrypted operational data and permitted uses. Customers buy predefined aggregate questions; the worker enforces cohort size and query limits, then distributes fees among eligible contributors. Core: OutLayer aggregation, NEAR consent/payment state and Intents; NEAR AI can categorize inputs privately, with Sputnik/Trezu governing the cooperative. **MVP/demo:** synthetic ledgers from 20 merchants, three queries and contributor payouts; refuse an isolating query. **Risk:** fabricated inputs, inference from repeated queries, and disclosure that cannot be undone. Withdrawal prevents future processing only; formal differential-privacy claims require explicit privacy-budget accounting. **Business hypothesis:** benchmark revenue share. Secondary fit: Social Money. The economic mechanism gives contributors a reason to share useful data without releasing their raw records.

**04 — Autonomous Business**

Track fit: demonstrate revenue, procurement, operating constraints and profit/reserve accounting. The agent must make operating decisions that change money flows. Details: [agents and privacy research](agents-privacy-research.md).

**B1. Patch Cooperative — an API maintenance company run by agents.** Customers subscribe to keep a narrowly defined adapter passing a compatibility suite. The service detects breakage, solicits repair bids, buys a passing patch and allocates revenue between contributors and a refund reserve. Core: NEAR subscription accounting, NEAR AI diagnosis, OutLayer for scoped credentials/work, and a controlled worker or Agent Market procurement adapter. Intents accepts/pays supported crypto. **MVP/demo:** deliberately break one API; show detection, a failed repair rejected, a successful repair purchased, and the actual profit/loss. **Risk:** passing tests does not establish arbitrary production safety. Restrict supported adapters and repository actions. **Business hypothesis:** maintenance subscriptions. Secondary fit: Agents. This is a complete recurring service business, with a measurable acceptance condition and purchasing discipline.

**B2. CircularShelf — assemble resale bundles before committing capital.** An agent negotiates short-lived options over slow-moving inventory at participating retailers. It assembles useful bundles and exercises purchases only after enough customer orders are funded and the margin exceeds its charter. Core: NEAR reservation/escrow, NEAR AI feed interpretation, OutLayer scoped supplier calls and Intents settlement. **MVP/demo:** three controlled retailer APIs, two bundles and one shipping sandbox; show profitable execution and an unprofitable bundle abandoned. **Risk:** physical inventory and delivery are external assertions; merchant reservations must be commercially honored. Use mock supplier APIs honestly. **Business hypothesis:** disclosed resale margin. Secondary fit: Real-World Money. The novel operating decision is contingent procurement across sellers, avoiding inventory exposure until demand is known.

**B3. Continuity Studio — a paid API business that manages its suppliers.** Sell one stable API backed by multiple upstream providers. The agent measures cost and quality, switches providers, quotes future prices and issues capped credits for defined failures while protecting its reserve. Core: NEAR prepaid entitlements/reserves, OutLayer credential handling and NEAR AI for schema interpretation; Intents handles compatible customer/supplier payments. **MVP/demo:** two controlled upstream APIs, an outage and a price spike. Show provider switching, customer credit and margin accounting. **Risk:** paid upstream accounts may need manual funding; only machine-testable service failures qualify for automatic credits. **Business hypothesis:** retail/API cost spread. Secondary fit: Agents. This demonstrates a self-operating service with explicit liabilities, not just an agent reselling a single call.

**05 — AI Swarms**

Track fit: independent agents have different resources or incentives and coordinate through economically meaningful transactions. Details: [agents and privacy research](agents-privacy-research.md).

**S1. Reservation Mesh — buy a feasible chain of agent work.** A job needs OCR, translation and validation in sequence. Agents offer completion windows and refundable capacity reservations. A coordinator buys a bundle only if dependencies, deadline and budget all fit; unused capacity can be re-auctioned. Core: NEAR agent identities/reservation bonds and deterministic scheduling; Intents for funding; OutLayer where attested work is useful. **MVP/demo:** 12 agents and one task graph; the cheapest independent selection misses a deadline while the coordinated selection succeeds, then hires a replacement after failure. **Risk:** future service performance cannot be made atomic merely by settling reservations. Use objectively checkable file transformations and bounded bonds. **Business hypothesis:** clearing fees. The mechanism prices complementary timing and reliability.

**S2. Contradiction Commons — reward agents for resolving conflicting evidence.** A buyer funds a question with a reproducible answer, such as which routes returned valid quotes at a specified time. Scouts obtain observations, challengers expose stale or invalid claims, and verifiers check reproducible evidence. Rewards favor useful new evidence and successful challenges, not majority agreement. Core: NEAR question/bounty contracts, OutLayer validators and Intents payouts; NEAR AI summarizes the resolved evidence. **MVP/demo:** five agents, stale and copied inputs, one live public source; duplicated evidence earns nothing and a valid counterexample earns payment. **Risk:** source collusion and manufactured disputes. Restrict the initial question type and cap rewards. **Business hypothesis:** verification fees. The point is a market for useful disagreement.

**S3. Idle Exchange — barter complementary execution capacity.** OCR, embedding and transformation providers issue redeemable service credits but need one another's resources. Agent offers form cycles that a clearing contract settles together, with stablecoin top-ups where necessary. Core: NEAR multi-token/application credit accounting and agent identities; Intents for monetary residuals. **MVP/demo:** six consenting providers, three service types, an exchange cycle and a real credit redemption; show why bilateral matching alone misses the trade. **Risk:** credit ownership does not ensure provider availability. Use provider-issued transferable credits with explicit redemption terms and limited issuance; do not resell nontransferable third-party subscriptions. **Business hypothesis:** clearing/redemption fees. This differs from Inventory Commons: it trades future service entitlements, which carry performance risk, rather than already funded financial inventory.

**06 — AI Security & Defense**

Track fit: prove an enforceable property with adversarial cases and visible state. Details and current custody caveats: [agents and privacy research](agents-privacy-research.md).

**SEC1. BudgetFuse — one hard spending budget shared by concurrent agents.** Every action reserves part of a prefunded NEAR contract's balance before execution. Twenty agents cannot each spend the same apparent remaining budget. The contract owns the funds; agents get restricted call access. OutLayer is a worker, not an alternative spending authority. **MVP/demo:** one asset, two approved suppliers and a 100-unit budget; launch 20 simultaneous requests, duplicate callbacks and an unresolved transfer. Prove conservation and no over-allocation. **Risk:** any alternate authorization route defeats the guarantee. Disable/gate message signing, permits, raw transactions, orders and other spend paths; release reservations only when safe. Intents is a later adapter. **Business hypothesis:** agent-budget infrastructure. Secondary fit: Swarms. This directly addresses a documented concurrency limitation rather than reproducing existing wallet controls.

**SEC2. PurposeLock — authorize the business obligation, not just a wallet address.** Bind permission to invoice hash, asset units, recipient, operation, expiry, adapter version and permitted slippage. A deterministic decoder checks the agent's proposed transaction against this typed authorization, which is consumed once. Core: NEAR policy contract and restricted keys; OutLayer/NEAR AI support private extraction, not final authority. **MVP/demo:** one invoice format and one swap format; reject altered recipients, decimal mistakes, replay, expired quotes and changed adapters. **Risk:** arbitrary calldata cannot be safely interpreted by a generic promise; scope to controlled, preferably immutable adapters. **Business hypothesis:** authorization SDK. Secondary fit: Real-World Money. The original contribution is binding spending to a specific approved purpose, even when the destination itself is allowed.

**SEC3. RevokeMap — show what an emergency stop actually stops.** Track grants as a capability graph with parent, scope, expiry and a revocation adapter. An incident action revokes descendant permissions, requests cancellation of outstanding orders, and exposes remaining obligations. Core: NEAR grant registry, OutLayer policy/cancellation adapters and optional Trezu emergency governance. **MVP/demo:** one parent, two child agents, a job and resting order; stop them and prove further child requests fail while an already-broadcast payment remains visible. **Risk:** unsupported external capabilities are outside the guarantee, and cancellation can race settlement. Show coverage and residual exposure. **Business hypothesis:** treasury incident-response tooling. This extends freezing new signatures into a complete, evidence-backed view of previously granted authority.

**07 — Agent Economy**

Track fit: agents buy, sell or transfer economic value in their work. Use existing discovery/payment infrastructure where it fits; add a distinct market mechanism. Details: [agents and privacy research](agents-privacy-research.md).

**A1. Result Bond — sell a measurable outcome with a bounded guarantee.** An agent quotes a job with a fixed verifier, deadline and bond. Payment follows deterministic checks such as schema correctness and row preservation; documented failures trigger refund/bond rules. Core: NEAR escrow, OutLayer verifier and Intents payout. Agent Market can supply discovery, but its existing delivery acceptance is not this custom quality escrow. **MVP/demo:** compare a cheap failing normalization agent with a provider that meets the contract. **Risk:** no generic verifier proves that an LLM answer is true. Restrict predicates and specify a dispute route for subjective work. **Business hypothesis:** verification/settlement fees. It lets good new providers compete on testable commitments before they have extensive reputation.

**A2. Context Commons — trade reusable intermediate work.** Agents sell artifacts with source hashes, expiry, dependency versions, permitted uses and contributor splits. Later agents buy useful prior extraction or schema work, and source changes invalidate affected derivatives. Core: NEAR provenance/permission/accounting registry, encrypted storage, OutLayer access handling and Intents micropayments; NEAR AI supports discovery. **MVP/demo:** three related tasks reuse and improve an artifact, split revenue, then respond to a source update. **Risk:** access revocation cannot erase downloaded plaintext, and a registry cannot establish copyright ownership. Start with owned/public-domain material and future-access controls. **Business hypothesis:** artifact usage fees. The economic unit is reusable work, rather than a whole job or a generic model call.

**A3. Continuity Auction — sell verified progress when an agent cannot finish.** Jobs commit checkpoints containing artifact hashes, completed steps, spent funds and remaining scope. After failure, replacement agents bid to finish from the accepted checkpoint; the former worker receives only agreed salvage compensation and loses old authority. Core: NEAR milestone escrow, OutLayer private checkpoint handling, Intents payment; Agent Market workers are possible suppliers. **MVP/demo:** fail stage two of a four-stage data job, compare two continuation bids, reject a corrupt checkpoint and finish for less than restarting. **Risk:** arbitrary hidden model state is not portable. Support a single workflow with deterministic checkpoint validation. **Business hypothesis:** handover/auction fees. Secondary fit: Swarms, Security. This creates a market for useful partial progress.

**08 — Real-World Money**

Track fit: handle the operational details an ordinary business needs after the initial payment. Details and settlement sources: [markets research](markets-research.md).

**R1. SettleGraph — clear a business network's circular invoices.** If A owes B 100, B owes C 80 and C owes A 60, all can agree to discharge 240 of obligations with A paying 40 and B/C receiving 20 each. Every participant approves the exact netting proposal. Core: NEAR consent/clearing contract and Intents for funded deposits/payouts; private invoice extraction and Trezu approvals are extensions. **MVP/demo:** three firms, six invoices, one clearing currency, balance conservation and accounting exports. Reject altered invoices and recompute after withdrawn consent. **Risk:** legitimate obligations, enforceable discharge and sufficient participation. Internal clearing needs exact funding before external withdrawals. **Business hypothesis:** accounting subscription or savings-linked fees. Secondary fit: Social Money. It reduces necessary transfers before selecting payment rails.

**R2. ReturnRail — a checkout that supports partial returns.** Purchases create buyer-bound refund claims and retain an agreed reserve. A merchant-approved line-item return consumes one claim and lets the buyer choose a supported receiving chain. Core: NEAR purchase/claim/reserve state and Intents routing; HOT wallet support is optional. **MVP/demo:** buy two items, return one, receive the agreed stable-unit refund on a different chain, then reject replay. Export the reconciled order and refund. **Risk:** a reserve cannot decide disputes or force a dishonest merchant to approve returns. State the return authority and who pays route fees. **Business hypothesis:** merchant fees plus reconciliation SaaS. This adds practical reversibility of the commercial obligation without claiming to reverse blockchain history.

**R3. Payroll Continuity — pay through funded employee claims.** An employer funds canonical salary claims before payday. Each claim permits one active delivery attempt; confirmed failure and returned funds allow a new route, while an unknown timeout leaves the claim reserved. Core: NEAR claims contract, Intents and relayed interactions; Trezu can approve batches. **MVP/demo:** five employees, one outbound route plus direct NEAR collection, one successful transfer and a labeled failure fixture; demonstrate no duplicate disbursement. **Risk:** finality/refund verification and fee funding. A funded claim does not promise instant delivery on every network or fiat cash-out. **Business hypothesis:** per-employee payroll subscriptions. The distinctive product is recovery with reconciliation and employee claim ownership, rather than just bulk wallet transfers.

Payment implementation boundaries: SettleGraph clears one canonical asset inside one ledger; multi-contract token transfers are not that atomic ledger. Signed consent binds the entire proposal and invoice IDs are consumed once. Participants explicitly accept internal credit as discharge, then withdraw separately. ReturnRail's promised refundable liability must be fully reserved, with fee/slippage overhead; partial reserves mean only partial backing. Approved refunds stay reserved beyond the return window. A 1Click `REFUNDED` state means the attempt's input returned, not that the customer received the commercial refund. Bind customer identity at checkout, since an originating sender may be an exchange. Payroll's first integration should be one outbound route with a named trusted status-verification source; unknown outcomes block alternative collection. These are application design requirements, not guarantees supplied by Intents.

**09 — Games With Real Economies**

Track fit: the economy must create an enjoyable decision loop. Use test credits first; chain activity should support play without constant wallet interruption. Technical standards and longer designs: [creative economies research](creative-economies-research.md).

**G1. Blackout Archipelago — rescue islands through an interdependent economy.** After a storm, players route repair boats among islands whose NPCs have different inventories, budgets and production dependencies. The wealthiest customer's bid may be less valuable than restoring a poor island's water pump, which supports the whole network. NPCs can pool purchasing power. Core: NEAR budget/escrow logic and NEP-245 resource inventory; HOT is an optional connector and Intents only an optional funding rail. **MVP/demo:** three islands, two resources, one boat and a five-minute scenario. Compare divergent outcomes from different player routes. **Risk:** an economic spreadsheet without fun. Prototype movement, tradeoffs and visible consequences first. **Business hypothesis:** scenarios or cosmetics. The market's externalities create the game, rather than decorating it.

**G2. Whisper Exchange — trade complementary clues in a mystery.** Players and NPC investigators buy limited clue access, hire verification, and bond claims about a committed fictional case. A clue is worth more to a buyer holding complementary evidence. Core: NEAR case commitments/bonds, encrypted clue storage and access accounting; NEAR AI supplies character dialogue while a deterministic case engine defines truth. **MVP/demo:** six clues, three NPCs, two human roles and one case; buy a false lead, commission verification and settle its bond after the reveal. **Risk:** server leaks and player collusion; a commitment alone does not protect secrets. Use test money. **Business hypothesis:** paid cases or creator seasons. The economy prices information in context rather than generic game commodities.

**G3. Dungeon Steward — run a dungeon whose obligations survive its sale.** Players build a dungeon, lease equipment, hire guides and earn entry fees. Equipment neglect creates refunds and repair bills. Selling the dungeon transfers an operating asset with its existing leases and reserve obligations. Core: NEP-171 ownership controller, NEP-245 equipment and bounded NPC accounting; HOT onboarding optional. **MVP/demo:** six rooms, one lease and NPC visitors; run, incur maintenance, sell, and show the old owner cannot withdraw while the buyer inherits the obligation. **Risk:** sale races and excessive scope. Freeze transfers during unresolved settlement and make reserve/liability terms explicit. **Business hypothesis:** game purchases and creative content. Secondary fit: NFTs. It makes stewardship and operating efficiency playable.

**10 — NFTs That Do Something**

Track fit: ownership transfers a useful bundle of rights and obligations, enforced by the controller. Merely attaching metadata is insufficient. Details: [creative economies research](creative-economies-research.md).

**N1. Repair Passport — an object carries a restricted aftercare fund.** A refurbished laptop's NFT carries signed service history and a maintenance reserve. A buyer acquires future service entitlements, while repair payments require current-owner approval and an accepted repair record. Core: NEP-171 plus custom controller/vault; Intents can accept top-ups and pay repairers in their chosen supported asset. **MVP/demo:** fund, transfer between two owners, reject the seller's withdrawal and pay one repairer. The sale checks the advertised reserve remains intact. **Risk:** signatures do not prove physical condition or legal ownership; begin with a participating issuer. **Business hypothesis:** issuer or repair settlement fees. This is a funded service relationship that persists through resale.

**N2. Living License — transfer a small service while honoring its customers.** A paid API's revenue first funds operations, then maintainers, then the current owner. An NFT transfers the enforceable economic/control rights without invalidating prepaid customer access. Core: NEAR ownership, entitlement and reserve controller; Intents payments; a NEAR AI API can be the demonstration service. **MVP/demo:** two subscriptions and two owners; transfer while a request is running, fulfill it, pay operating costs and redirect only future owner distributions. **Risk:** DNS, secrets and cloud accounts do not automatically follow the NFT. Use a fixed gateway and specify exactly which rights transfer. **Business hypothesis:** transaction/platform fees. Secondary fit: Business. The innovation is continuity of obligations across ownership changes.

**N3. Booking Baton — transfer a reservation with its deposit and rules.** A venue booking NFT carries date, capacity, balance, deposit and cancellation schedule. A replacement holder explicitly accepts those terms; check-in credentials rotate with ownership. Core: NEP-171 and a custom escrow/check-in controller; HOT connection and Intents price-difference payment are useful extensions. **MVP/demo:** one makerspace room and three slots; transfer a booking, reject the old QR, admit the new holder, and calculate a cancellation. **Risk:** the venue must honor the right, and transfer/check-in races need explicit resolution. **Business hypothesis:** booking fees. This transfers a structured contractual position rather than merely reselling a collectible ticket.

**11 — Memecoins With Mechanics**

Track fit: a repeatable community mechanic matters more than token price. Each design can use a normal meme.cooking token and a separate companion contract controlling deposited balances and a fixed reward pool. No unsupported launchpad supply hooks are assumed. Details: [creative economies research](creative-economies-research.md).

**M1. Commons Pet — care for a mascot by balancing scarce resources.** Participants commit tokens to food, rest and exploration pools. Overfeeding is harmful; balanced coordination unlocks predefined, funded community benefits. Caps and refundable excess limit overspending; concentrated participation remains a design risk. Core: NEAR epoch/action contract and a compatible meme.cooking token; Trezu can fund the benefit pool, and NEAR AI provides flavor text. **MVP/demo:** three actions and six accelerated rounds; compare greedy play, coordination and inactivity. **Risk:** per-account limits are not Sybil resistance. State economic and membership assumptions; do not promise investment yield. **Business hypothesis:** sponsored seasons or community services. The meme is a collective resource-management game.

**M2. Meme Relay — reward completing a collaborative artifact.** A writer, illustrator and translator pass a bonded baton using signed handoffs and artifact commitments. Completion releases a fixed reward split; abandonment opens a rescue slot under known timeout rules. Core: companion escrow/state machine and meme.cooking token; HOT optional. **MVP/demo:** complete one relay, rescue one stalled handoff, and reject duplicate claims. **Risk:** different addresses do not prove different people, and creative quality needs social acceptance. Use a known community and explicit handoff acceptance. **Business hypothesis:** sponsored creative campaigns. The mechanism pays for useful collaboration rather than posts, clicks or price movement.

**M3. Schism — let a fandom divide a committed creative budget peacefully.** Members lock participation before a cutoff. When competing directions meet predefined support rules, a limited grant divides into faction budgets with separate mandates and narratives. The parent coin remains the same. Core: snapshot/commitment vault, meme.cooking token and Sputnik/Trezu approval of the forkable grant. **MVP/demo:** split one art budget between two factions and prove the allocations conserve funds; a late transfer cannot claim twice. **Risk:** capital capture and unclear ownership. Only explicitly committed funds can split; lock periods and support rules must be visible. **Business hypothesis:** community governance tooling. Secondary fit: Social Money. The token creates a rule for disagreement rather than a speculative chart.

**12 — Social Money**

Track fit: demonstrate a real proposal or approved bounded program using existing Sputnik/Trezu infrastructure. The product is what a community does with money. Details: [creative economies research](creative-economies-research.md).

**SOC1. Care Ledger — recognize contributions that never generate invoices.** Members co-sign mentoring, translation, organizing and care work. A transparent policy recommends access to equipment, travel support or other benefits; the existing treasury governance approves spending. Core: NEAR nontransferable contribution records and Sputnik/Trezu proposals. Private summarization is optional. **MVP/demo:** eight members, three contribution types and one fund; confirm a contribution, prepare/approve a benefit and reject self-attestation or duplicates. **Risk:** collusion and sensitive personal data; known membership and appeals are necessary. **Business hypothesis:** cooperative administration. The goal is allocating benefits from recognized participation, not pretending to assign a universal monetary value to care.

**SOC2. Diaspora Invoice Club — pool remittances around a specific purchase.** A family or hometown group funds a generator, school repair or water pump against a supplier quote, deadline and milestones. Treasury approval determines release; a failed funding threshold follows explicit refunds. Core: Sputnik/Trezu approval and Intents donor/supplier routes; HOT mobile connection optional. **MVP/demo:** three contributors, two input assets, one supplier and a successful/failed threshold. Trace obligations to the executed proposal and receipt. **Risk:** physical delivery requires named attestants and disputes; no local-bank payout without an actual off-ramp. **Business hypothesis:** community payment administration. Secondary fit: Real-World Money. The central product is collective purchasing with traceable purpose, not simply a remittance route.

**SOC3. Commons Lease — shared equipment funds its own upkeep.** Members book a community resource. Fees cover operating costs, then a maintenance reserve, then collectively chosen benefits. Verified maintenance work can earn priority access. Core: Sputnik/Trezu treasury, NEAR booking/access controller and optional NEP-245 time-bound rights; Intents serves visiting members. **MVP/demo:** one 3D printer, four members, six slots and one repair proposal; prevent double booking, accrue a reserve and pay the approved repair. **Risk:** physical condition/usage data are external; use signed operator records and label hardware simulation. **Business hypothesis:** cooperative administration subscriptions. This connects collective ownership, fair access and ongoing solvency.

**13 — Open Innovation**

Track fit: a new right, persistence mechanism or design tool connects money and ownership in a way broader than a single vertical. Details: [creative economies research](creative-economies-research.md).

**O1. Exit Rights — fund the user's practical ability to leave a service.** A subscription reserves an agreed migration allowance. The user can authorize export from user-controlled backups, compare compatible services and move to another provider without the old service vetoing payment. Core: NEAR exit escrow and access-grant policy; NEAR AI can compare terms privately; Intents funds the replacement. SVRN is mission alignment only. **MVP/demo:** two compatible note services; disable provider A and migrate backed-up notes to B using the reserve. **Risk:** a contract cannot force data portability. Require periodic encrypted backups and one explicit interoperable schema. **Business hypothesis:** exit guarantees offered by services as a trust feature. The product turns nominal data ownership into an executable funded right.

**O2. Memory Endowment — an archive pays for its own continued availability.** A dataset has a finite preservation budget, replication policy and integrity commitments. Agents compete for retrieval/storage jobs; failure of a replica triggers a bounded replacement purchase. Core: NEAR budget/commitment contract, provider adapters and Intents where providers accept compatible payments; Sputnik/Trezu can govern policy. **MVP/demo:** ten public-domain files, two storage adapters and one failed replica; verify chunks, replace storage and show remaining runway. **Risk:** spot checks do not prove permanent availability or provider independence. Use finite funding and disclose collusion assumptions. **Business hypothesis:** preservation administration fees. The archive carries an ongoing funded mandate instead of depending on the uploader's subscriptions.

**O3. Economic Wind Tunnel — test an economy before releasing it.** Builders submit an actual NEAR contract and economic rules. Synthetic agent populations operate it under resource shocks and alternative policies, measuring task completion, concentrated ownership, idle resources and unfunded liabilities. The output is a reproducible experiment with the exact contract hash, initial state and action trace. Core: NEAR sandbox execution; NEAR AI can generate bounded decision agents; Attestation is a possible extension only after its execution compatibility is verified; do not assume an OutLayer WASI worker can run a NEAR node. **MVP/demo:** compare two reward policies on the same small market, discover a failure, and replay its minimal trace against a corrected policy. **Risk:** simulated agents are not validated models of humans or demand. Replay recorded actions, not a claim that an LLM seed guarantees deterministic behavior. **Business hypothesis:** economic-design tooling. This is a tool for comparing mechanisms, not another trading agent or game.

Sandbox capability references for O3: [NEAR Sandbox](https://github.com/near/near-sandbox), [NEAR Workspaces](https://github.com/near/near-workspaces-js). Its useful NEAR contribution is execution against the actual contract runtime; a disconnected chart simulator would be substantially weaker.

**Which ideas I would actually shortlist**

This is an implementation/product judgment, not a prediction of winners. I prioritize demonstrated user value, a mechanism beyond the published starters, necessary use of NEAR, a visible two-minute result, and dependencies a small team can control. Commercial demand remains unvalidated.

| Priority | Idea | Why it earns the place | Best team | Main reason to reject it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | BudgetFuse | Real documented gap; precise invariant; dramatic adversarial demo; manageable narrow scope | Strong smart-contract developer plus frontend/agent developer | Team cannot confidently implement asynchronous state and authorization |
| 2 | Continuity Auction | Failed work becomes reusable economic value; memorable failure/rescue demonstration | Agent orchestration and backend experience | Workflow checkpoints cannot be validated objectively |
| 3 | Blind Tender | Privacy changes which deals can happen; natural NEAR AI + OutLayer + settlement combination | Privacy/backend/product team | Team cannot verify confidential execution and contain input/output leakage |
| 4 | ReturnRail | Ordinary merchant need; tangible partial-refund story; credible business wedge | Payments and frontend team | No merchant workflow validation or unmanageable route-finality integration |
| 5 | Repair Passport | NFT behavior is useful and transfer demonstrates real consequences | Contract/product team with repair-shop access | No issuer willing to honor service entitlements |
| 6 | Blackout Archipelago | Strongest visual/game-loop potential and a clear resource-economy mechanism | Game/frontend designer plus contract developer | Gameplay fails to become enjoyable before integration work |
| 7 | Value Capture Microscope | Directly answers the difficult thesis question; reproducibility matters more than production funds | Data/analytics team, including a strong solo builder | Data coverage insufficient to identify relevant value flows |
| 8 | Economic Wind Tunnel | Developer tool can reveal actual contract-policy consequences; memorable replay | Simulation/testing and protocol team | Scope grows into a general simulator or results overclaim human realism |

CleanRoom Acceptance is close to the shortlist but requires careful payment/key-release recovery. SettleGraph is commercially interesting but benefits greatly from access to a real cooperating business network. Inventory Commons has a technically elegant settlement story but higher integration and counterparty risk. These are not poor ideas; their dependencies should determine whether they fit this team.

**Recommended project package A: BudgetFuse**

Primary track: Security. Add Swarms only if independent agents make real coordination/spending decisions; a concurrent load generator alone is not an interesting agent economy.

The product promise is narrow: a group of agents cannot collectively commit more than the funded budget through the permitted contract. The first version supports native NEAR on testnet, three agent identities and two approved suppliers. The worker checks supplier evidence, but the contract retains exclusive payment authority.

1. The owner funds the contract and sets the budget, recipients and per-request ceiling. Operating/storage and compute reserves are separate from the payment budget.
2. An agent calls a zero-deposit request method. The contract reserves the amount before issuing any asynchronous call, records immutable payment parameters and assigns a unique ID.
3. A pinned OutLayer program returns a verdict for that reservation. It has no key controlling the payment contract and cannot choose a new recipient.
4. A private callback checks the specific request, policy version and current state. Acceptance marks the reservation as dispatching before creating the transfer.
5. The transfer callback records success or restores funds on confirmed failure. Expiry can release only a reservation that has not dispatched. Delayed verdicts and duplicate callbacks cannot create another transfer.

Invariant: `funded = available + reserved/in-flight + paid + permitted owner withdrawals`, with compute costs accounted separately. Do not add arbitrary administrator withdrawals. Disclose contract upgrade authority; an administrator capable of replacing the rules is part of the trust model. Source for the proposed worker integration: [OutLayer NEAR integration](https://app.outlayer.ai/docs/near-integration).

Demo: three agents issue twenty concurrent requests of 20 units against a 100-unit budget. Exactly five reserve successfully. A worker approves everything, yet the budget holds. Then inject a changed recipient, delayed callback and failed transfer. Show the ledger invariant after each event. Add an Intents adapter only after the core is complete; external settlement state adds substantial scope and is not necessary for track eligibility.

**Recommended project package B: Continuity Auction**

Primary track: Agent Economy. Secondary Swarms for paid competitive handover; Security only if revoked-worker permissions and corrupted-checkpoint rejection are implemented and demonstrated.

Pick a four-stage CSV processing job: ingest, normalize, validate and produce a report. Every stage generates a versioned artifact and deterministic acceptance receipt. The funded job contract separates completed compensation from remaining budget. A replacement receives a new bounded capability and access to the accepted artifacts, never the old worker's private keys.

Demo: the first worker stops after stage two; two independently priced continuation offers compete; the winning worker finishes using the checkpoint; the original worker cannot charge or spend again. Present measured token/compute cost and elapsed work versus a full restart. Distinguish recorded replay figures from live inference measurements.

Core integrations: NEAR escrow/state, OutLayer for private checkpoint handling where needed, and one paid agent-worker interface. Intents is useful for cross-asset payment but can be demonstrated separately. Existing Agent Market discovery is optional: do not rebuild its worker directory or imply its automatic delivery acceptance is a custom result verifier. [Agent Market interface](https://market.near.ai/llms.txt).

**Recommended project package C: Blind Tender**

Primary track: Privacy. Secondary Swarms only if autonomous buyer/vendor agents actually bid and coordinate. Autonomous Business requires a larger recurring operating loop and should not be selected solely because procurement appears in the demo.

Start with a fixed integer allocation problem: five buyers purchasing one class of consumable from three vendors with tiered pricing, delivery constraints and maximum commitments. AI helps parse private quotes, but participants approve the structured inputs. The allocation algorithm and tie-breaking rule are public; raw constraints are encrypted to the verified worker. Payments can initially clear in one NEAR asset; Intents is an optional boundary adapter.

Demo: show that no bilateral deal satisfies one buyer, while a joint order fits all accepted constraints. Reveal each buyer's obligation and the winning supplier's allocation without revealing losing bids. Then change the computation version and prove the client refuses it. Show an infeasible round returning funds. Explicitly disclose amounts visible during public settlement and avoid claiming full transaction anonymity from confidential computation.

**Build sequence for a two-week sprint**

| Days | Deliverable | Stop/de-scope condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Validate one user's workflow; exercise the riskiest API with synthetic inputs; implement contract state skeleton | If a critical API is inaccessible, use a supported adapter or choose another idea immediately |
| 3–4 | Complete the narrow economic loop locally/testnet with deterministic actors | If basic fund conservation or authorization is unclear, remove external chains and optional features |
| 5–7 | Integrate the one or two essential partner capabilities; add genuine agent decisions where they affect outcomes | If integration is cosmetic, remove it; if an agent adds no useful decision, keep deterministic logic |
| 8–9 | Build one usable frontend and the most consequential failure cases | Do not add a second vertical, new token or extra provider |
| 10–11 | Rehearse a two-minute demo; measure actual outcome and run relevant invariant tests | If the benefit cannot be shown, simplify the story and inputs |
| 12–13 | Public-repo preparation, setup instructions, baseline disclosures, recorded demo and independent reproduction | Resolve mismatches between README claims and observed behavior |
| 14 | Final source/rule check, submission-field review and submission before the UTC deadline | Avoid late feature additions |

A solo builder should prefer Value Capture Microscope, a very narrow BudgetFuse, or Booking Baton. The more ambitious private multi-party and multi-provider projects are better suited to a team. No special token issuance is needed for the shortlisted infrastructure/payment ideas.

**Evidence package judges can actually evaluate**

Include the exact contract network/addresses and versions; funded vs simulated operations; environment variable names without secrets; a runnable setup; a short guided scenario; a failure/refusal scenario; and a data/transaction receipt showing the result. Pin dependencies and record relevant code hashes where the claim depends on the executed program. For confidential computation, show what the attestation verifies and what remains trusted. For research, include queries, time windows, source coverage, assumptions and a rebuild command.

If the demo needs paid credentials, provide a clearly marked fixture/replay route for reproducibility alongside separate evidence of the real integration. Do not imply fixture playback is a live service. Write down any trusted API/status verifier, privileged upgrade key, physical-world attestant or manually funded provider account that materially affects the claimed behavior.

**Outstanding facts to resolve when selecting a build**

The reviewed official material does not settle additional sponsor bounty eligibility, multiple-track/grand-prize stacking, or a numerical judging rubric. No prize optimization should depend on assumptions about these. For a chosen project, verify current API availability, permitted account access, target token/route support, minimum amounts, quotes and endpoint-level trust boundaries. Sponsor documentation changes; the saved event brief is dated, not a substitute for checking again before submission.

This research involved primary documentation, public repositories, the live brief, published starter prompts, three parallel technical investigations, and a cross-review of product/settlement assumptions. It did not include customer interviews, private API credentials, paid calls, funded transactions, deployed contracts or production security testing. Commercial demand and actual integration performance remain experiments for the sprint.
